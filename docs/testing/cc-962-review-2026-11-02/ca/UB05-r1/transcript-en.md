And what about industrial processes and agriculture and land use? Does a missing value mean that emissions there are zero?

**Climate Advisor**

No. **A missing value does not mean zero emissions** — it means that no data or assessment have yet been entered for these categories.

In the inventory for 2024:
- **IPPU / industrial processes and product use (IV):** 2 of 2 required items are missing.
- **AFOLU / agriculture, forestry and other land use (V):** 3 of 3 required items are missing.

Before the report is passed on, it is necessary to determine for each item whether:
1. it occurs and requires an emissions estimate,
2. it **does not occur** within the city — in which case it can be marked as “not occurring”, with a brief justification,
3. the data cannot be obtained or estimated — in which case this should be clearly documented in accordance with the inventory methodology.

Only a confirmed “not occurring” can justify the absence of emissions in a given category; empty fields should not automatically be treated as zero.

### IV. Industrial processes and product use (IPPU)
Above all, collect information on activities that cause emissions **other than from fuel combustion**:

- **Industrial plants and technological processes:** the type of plant and production, annual production or raw material consumption (e.g. cement, lime, glass, metals, chemicals), data on processes that may emit CO₂ or other gases.
- **Refrigerants / F-gases:** the number and type of refrigeration and air conditioning equipment, the types and quantities of refrigerants used, servicing, losses and refrigerant recovery — particularly in larger retail facilities, cold storage warehouses, hospitals and plants.
- **Data sources:** city hall (environment, business activity or construction departments), the national/regional statistical office, registers of environmental permits and notifications, plant operators, refrigeration servicing companies and the relevant national inventory report.

If there are no industrial processes covered by this category within the city, document this, for example by reviewing the business register and environmental permits. Do not automatically assume that refrigeration emissions are also absent.

### V. Agriculture, forestry and other land use (AFOLU)
The data needed cover three groups:

- **Livestock:** numbers by species and husbandry system; data on manure management. This enables methane from enteric fermentation and manure management to be estimated.
- **Land and land-use changes:** the area of forests, arable land, grassland, wetlands and built-up land, and changes between these categories in the reporting year and previous years. Tree felling, planting and land reclamation are also important.
- **Non-CO₂ emissions from land:** mineral and organic fertilizer consumption, crop area, possibly rice cultivation or agricultural residue burning — if they occur locally.
- **Data sources:** the local or regional statistical office, land and building records, the surveying/cadastre office, the spatial planning department, the forestry administration, the agriculture office, satellite and land-cover data, and surveys of larger farms.

### Where to check and fill in the gaps in CityCatalyst
1. Open the **2024** inventory.
2. Go to the emissions category/sector view.
3. Open **IV — IPPU** and **V — AFOLU** in turn — all items in these sectors are currently unfilled: **2 of 2** and **3 of 3**, respectively.
4. For each item, check whether you can add activity data and a source, enter an estimate or mark the category as **“not occurring”** with a justification. Do not select a zero value merely because data are not yet available.

Methodologically, emissions are most often calculated as **activity data × emission factor**; local and national data from reliable sources are preferred. IPPU includes, among other things, industrial processes and the release of gases from products such as refrigerants, while AFOLU covers animals, land and changes in its use, and emissions associated with fertilization.
