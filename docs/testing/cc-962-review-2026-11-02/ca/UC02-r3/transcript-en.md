## Turn 2

**User**

Let's look at the SEEG proposal for residential buildings, scope 1 (I.1.1). Where do the data come from, what year are they from, and what value do you propose?

**Climate Advisor**

For **residential buildings — scope 1 (I.1.1)**, the proposed source is **SEEG (Sistema de Estimativa de Emissão de Gases do Efeito Estufa)**.

- **Data coverage:** national for Brazil
- **Data year:** **2015**
- **Proposed value:** **1,117,504,701 kgCO₂e** (approx. **1.12 MtCO₂e**, 100-year horizon)

The value includes CO₂, CH₄ and N₂O from fuel combustion in residential buildings. The source data do not provide an activity value or its unit.

## Turn 3

**User**

Do these data match our city and this category? What cannot be determined from them?

**Climate Advisor**

Partially.

- **Category:** yes — SEEG data are assigned precisely to **I.1.1: Residential buildings, scope 1, fuel combustion within the city boundary**.
- **City:** not fully — the source has a **national (Brazil)** match, rather than directly to São Paulo.
- **Year:** no — the proposal is based on data from **2015**, while the inventory concerns **2022**.

The following cannot be reliably determined from this:
- whether the value reflects actual fuel consumption in São Paulo in 2022;
- how emissions are distributed across fuels, building types or districts;
- what the energy activity was (e.g. the quantity of fuel consumed) — the source does not provide an activity value or unit;
- whether local changes in fuels, energy efficiency or building structure have occurred since 2015.

It therefore matches **thematically**, but has limited **temporal and geographic** representativeness.
