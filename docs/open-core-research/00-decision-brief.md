# Decision brief: the open core as digital public infrastructure

Open Earth Foundation, 1 October 2026. Three pages. Depth is in the numbered documents alongside this one.

## 1. The question

CityCatalyst's codebase will supply the foundation of the GCoM Digital Service from July 2027. OEF wants that same foundation to serve other municipal networks and other domains (health, biodiversity, water) as digital public infrastructure: shared systems that others build on, addressing data sovereignty, tool and data interoperability, and the governance of AI agents acting on public data. Three questions follow. Is CityCatalyst's codebase the right starting point, or should the core be started fresh or assembled from existing mature components? What exactly is "the core"? And how is it extracted from the monorepo without disturbing the GCoM build?

## 2. What exists today, in one paragraph

The codebase is a Next.js application that is both front end and API, plus a Python data catalogue service, a Python agent service and two Python prioritisation services, deployed on Kubernetes with a develop, test and production ladder ([01-component-inventory.md](01-component-inventory.md)). The infrastructure-like parts are thinner than the recommendation deck suggests. Login is email and password with authenticator-app multi-factor authentication added in September ([app/src/lib/2fa.ts](../../app/src/lib/2fa.ts)). The OAuth 2.0 server issues tokens with two scopes, `read` and `write`, signed with a shared secret, and has no OpenID Connect, no client registration and no revocation ([app/src/app/api/v1/token/route.ts](../../app/src/app/api/v1/token/route.ts)). Tenancy is Organization, Project, City, Inventory with no programme entity and no role on city membership ([app/src/models/CityUser.ts](../../app/src/models/CityUser.ts)). The only entity identifier is UN/LOCODE, a transport location code with no boundaries and no history ([app/src/models/City.ts](../../app/src/models/City.ts)). Spatially, the core stores one polygon per city as text and runs one point-in-polygon query ([global-api/routes/legacy/city_boundaries_endpoint.py](../../global-api/routes/legacy/city_boundaries_endpoint.py)). There is no general audit log, no policy engine and no workflow engine. Three pieces are already close to domain-agnostic: the signed webhook outbox, the GDPR consent and retention module merged on 28 September, and the document ingestion path with its input catalogue. The SDK pipeline watches a path that does not exist, so no SDK has been generated.

## 3. What the core is, and is not

**The core is** a thin, generic layer that owns eight interfaces and nothing else: identity federation; the record envelope; the entity registry with spatial geometry; consent and policy; the agent registry and audit log; events; the catalogue; and the spatial index ([02-target-architecture.md](02-target-architecture.md)). Five primitives give those interfaces their shape: a principal and mandate model (people, organisations, services and agents, with explicit "acts for X in role Y" records); a signed record envelope; a place and jurisdiction entity with versioned geometry and a typed, dated edge graph for nesting and overlap; a consent and policy record; and an agent record with a hash-chained action log.

**The core is not** the climate apps (inventory, risk, actions and plans, the energy model, the advisor's content), nor any service's front end, nor a reimplementation of identity servers, policy engines, workflow engines, catalogues or webhook delivery that already exist under foundation governance. Domain knowledge lives in a configuration package per domain: schemas, taxonomies, criteria, workflows and catalogue sources. The core carries no climate vocabulary.

## 4. Build on CityCatalyst's codebase, start fresh, or integrate

The benchmark against mature alternatives ([04-research-directions/08-component-benchmark.md](04-research-directions/08-component-benchmark.md)) gives a clear answer: evolve CityCatalyst's codebase, but under a rule that nothing the core keeps in-house may already be solved by a mature, foundation-governed component. No existing platform supplies the target. DIGIT, FIWARE and a GovStack composition each cover roughly 30 to 45 percent of it, and each is built around a different primary object (a citizen service request, a live sensor entity, a national registry) than the one OEF needs: a verifiable record about a place, held by a member of a network. That slot is open.

| Strategy | Time to a v1 a second network can use | Effort, estimate ±50% | Risk to the July 2027 MVP |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Evolve the codebase, strangler pattern, integrate mature parts | 15 to 20 months | 60 to 90 engineer-months | Low to medium |
| B. Fresh thin core, port the apps | 18 to 24 months | 90 to 140 engineer-months | High: two codebases in flight |
| C. Adopt DIGIT, FIWARE or a GovStack composition | 24 months or more | 100 to 160 engineer-months | High: unknown platform |

Estimates assume one senior engineer per engineer-month and a team of four to six; they are for comparison, not pricing.

What would be rebuilt if kept in-house, and should be retired or wrapped: the OAuth server (Keycloak or Ory), webhook delivery (Svix or the Standard Webhooks signature), client-side entitlements (OpenFGA for membership plus Cedar for rules, behind one AuthZEN-shaped call), catalogue metadata (CKAN or at least DCAT-AP output), job orchestration (Temporal), and the MCP server's own authentication and logging (an Apache-licensed gateway). What is rare and worth protecting: agentic ingestion of municipal documents into provenance-carrying structured records; the climate data pipelines and warehouse; country-scale programme rollouts across thousands of municipalities; and tenancy-aware exposure of a record store to AI agents through the Model Context Protocol (MCP). No digital public good found does the first of these end to end for sub-national data.

## 5. The extraction path

Strangler pattern, nothing breaks for the GCoM build ([03-extraction-roadmap.md](03-extraction-roadmap.md)). Phase 0, before the 23 October gate: publish the package boundary and the app integration rules; fix the SDK pipeline; add licence metadata per service. Phase 1, Q4 2026, the foundations the GCoM build needs anyway: the record envelope and schema registry; the place and jurisdiction entity with crosswalks beside LOCODE; the principal and mandate model with an OpenID Connect-conformant provider; the agent record and audit log in front of the MCP server; per-tenant export, access log and residency as configuration. Phase 2, Q1 to Q2 2027: swap the identity server, the policy engine and webhook delivery behind the interfaces; generalise events and the module registry; spatial index and OGC API Features. Phase 3, after the MVP: the domain configuration package, the federation entity configuration, conformance tests, and a second network as the proof.

## 6. Three decisions

1. **Foundation.** Evolve CityCatalyst's codebase into the core under the no-rebuild rule, with the five primitives as the Q4 2026 foundations scope. The alternative, a fresh thin core, costs a third more and puts two codebases in flight during the MVP window. If you want a second opinion, a structured deliberation on "evolve under the no-rebuild rule versus fresh thin core" with the benchmark table as input is the right question to put to it.
2. **Domain boundary.** Domain lives in configuration, not code. The core carries schemas, taxonomies, criteria and workflows as versioned data per domain, and no climate vocabulary. This is what lets a health or biodiversity network join without a fork, and it is what the GCoM reporting schema already needs.
3. **Package and licence boundary.** Which packages are the AGPL-3.0 core, which are Apache-2.0 (SDK, connectors, app integration rules), and which schemas are published as an open specification; plus contribution terms for anyone who changes the core. The licence schedule of 9 September 2026 covers the GCoM components; the core needs the same per package.

Two items were taken out of the decision list after review. The identity posture (conformant OpenID Connect plus bring-your-own identity provider now, a federation trust anchor later, run by a network not by OEF) is not ripe for a decision and is framed as a study in [04-research-directions/01-government-identity.md](04-research-directions/01-government-identity.md). Badge and verification wording is a question for C40 and GCoM, in [05-open-questions.md](05-open-questions.md); the generic rule underneath it, that the core produces issuer proofs and stores verifier receipts as separate signed objects and never asserts content is correct, is in the target architecture.

## 7. Flags

**Against the agreed positions.** None of the research contradicts them. The v2 recommendation deck still uses "app store" on the slide where the recommendations align and on the front-end slide; the handoff note says to avoid the term with GCoM. The London sessions of 28 and 29 September recorded that the Brazil and Chile programmes converge on the unified core by 2028, which is more specific than "until their path is decided". The 28 September session kept OAuth as "not written in stone"; the research supports keeping it provided it becomes a conformant OpenID Connect provider with enterprise single sign-on, and the benchmark says the server itself should be a mature component rather than the in-house one.

**CLEARANCE.** Third-party facts that would need checking before leaving OEF are marked in each note. In particular: the deck's climate-DPI slide checks out in substance (the Climate DPG Collection announced at COP30 by Brazil's ministry with the DPGA and ITS Rio, formally launched 17 April 2026; OPIN launched February 2026 during the state visit to India; the UN Climate Change quote of 17 June 2026; the Antalya Pledge on AI announced 21 September 2026 with the ITU), but the MOSIP figure should read "14 national rollouts across 31 country engagements" and the phrase "world's first global DPI for climate action" for OPIN was not found in a primary source.
