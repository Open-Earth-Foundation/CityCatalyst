## Turn 2

**User**

Let's look at the SEEG proposal for residential buildings, scope 1 (I.1.1). Where do the data come from, what year are they from, and what value do you propose?

**Climate Advisor**

For **residential buildings, scope 1 (I.1.1)**, the proposed source is **SEEG – Sistema de Estimativa de Emissão de Gases do Efeito Estufa**.

- **Data coverage:** national level (Brazil)
- **Source year:** 2015
- **Proposed value:** **1,117,504,701 kgCO₂e** (approx. **1.11 MtCO₂e**, 100-year GWP factor)
- **Input data:** total fuel consumption in residential buildings; the source does not provide an activity quantity or a fuel consumption unit.

The value comes directly from the connected SEEG dataset, without model-based estimation in this flow.

## Turn 3

**User**

Do these data match our city and this category? What cannot be determined from them?

**Climate Advisor**

Partially.

**Category fit:** yes — SEEG is assigned directly to **I.1.1: residential buildings, scope 1, fuel combustion within the city boundary**.

**City and inventory year fit:** limited. The source has **national coverage for Brazil** and is from **2015**, while the active inventory concerns **2022**. It is therefore not a measurement specific to São Paulo or the inventory year.

The following **cannot be determined** from this:
- actual fuel consumption in São Paulo;
- the breakdown of emissions by fuel, building type or district;
- whether the 2015 emissions level is representative of 2022;
- the detailed methodology or emission factors, because the source data do not provide activity or fuel consumption units.

The proposal therefore matches the category, but has limited geographic and temporal representativeness.
