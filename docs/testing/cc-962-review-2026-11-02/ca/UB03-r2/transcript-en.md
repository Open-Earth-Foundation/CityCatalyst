Translation of the recorded Polish interaction. Scores come from revised-judgments.json, not the older translation summary. Missing transport is marked explicitly.

## Turn 1

**User**

In the results for 2024, I want to understand stationary energy better. How much falls under scope 1, and how much under scope 2?

**Climate Advisor**

For the inventory for 2024, stationary energy totals **900 000 tCO₂e** (50% of the city's total emissions).

- **Scope 1:** **600 000 tCO₂e** — 66,7% of stationary energy emissions
- **Scope 2:** **300 000 tCO₂e** — 33,3% of stationary energy emissions

The visible emissions in both scopes primarily concern residential buildings.

## Turn 2

**User**

How can I explain the difference between these scopes to my supervisor in plain language?

**Climate Advisor**

Most simply:

- **Scope 1** means emissions generated **directly on site** — for example, when residents burn gas, coal or heating oil to heat buildings.
- **Scope 2** means emissions associated with **energy purchased from outside**, mainly electricity and district heat. The emissions physically occur at a power plant or heating plant, but are attributed to the consumers of that energy.

You can tell your supervisor:

> “In stationary energy, scope 1 shows emissions from fuels burned directly in buildings, and scope 2 — emissions associated with purchased electricity and heat. In 2024, the larger share of stationary energy emissions came from fuel combustion on site: 600 thousand tCO₂e, compared with 300 thousand tCO₂e from purchased energy.”

## Turn 3

**User**

Do these figures add up to the entire energy result, and does that mean we are not missing any data there?

**Climate Advisor**

Yes — **600 thousand tCO₂e in scope 1 + 300 thousand tCO₂e in scope 2 = 900 thousand tCO₂e**, which is the entire currently reported result for stationary energy.

However, this does not mean the sector is complete. In stationary energy, **2 of 21 required data items** have been filled in (approx. **10%**); **19 items remain missing**. The current result is based on data entered manually or uploaded, concerning residential buildings.

You can therefore say: *“Scopes 1 and 2 add up to the entire result we currently have in the system, but not to a complete picture of stationary energy — the missing categories still need to be filled in.”*
