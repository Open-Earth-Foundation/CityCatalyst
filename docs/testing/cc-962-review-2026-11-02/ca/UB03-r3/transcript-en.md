## Turn 1

**User**

In the results for 2024, I want to understand stationary energy better. How much falls under scope 1, and how much under scope 2?

**Climate Advisor**

In the inventory for 2024, stationary energy totals **900 000 tCO₂e** (50% of the city's total emissions):

- **Scope 1:** **600 000 tCO₂e** — **66,7%** of stationary energy emissions  
- **Scope 2:** **300 000 tCO₂e** — **33,3%** of stationary energy emissions  

Both values primarily concern residential buildings.

## Turn 2

**User**

How can I explain the difference between these scopes to my supervisor in plain language?

**Climate Advisor**

Most simply:

- **Scope 1** means emissions generated **on site**, for example when gas, coal or heating oil is burned in buildings for heating and hot water.  
- **Scope 2** means emissions associated with **energy purchased from outside** — mainly electricity (and sometimes also purchased heat or cooling). The emissions physically occur at the energy producer, but we attribute them to the consumer who uses that energy.

You can tell your supervisor:

> “In stationary energy, scope 1 shows fuels burned directly in buildings, and scope 2 — emissions associated with electricity and other energy that buildings purchase. In 2024, about two thirds of these emissions came from fuel combustion on site, and one third from purchased electricity.”

In figures: **600 thousand tCO₂e in scope 1** and **300 thousand tCO₂e in scope 2**.

## Turn 3

**User**

Do these figures add up to the entire energy result, and does that mean we are not missing any data there?

**Climate Advisor**

Yes — **600 thousand tCO₂e (scope 1) + 300 thousand tCO₂e (scope 2) = 900 thousand tCO₂e**, which is the entire currently reported result for the stationary energy sector.

However, this does not mean the sector is complete. For stationary energy, **2 of 21 required data items** have been filled in (about **10%**), and **19 items are still missing**. These two entered items are data added manually or imported.

So: the sum of the scopes matches the current sector result, but the result may change once the missing categories are filled in.
