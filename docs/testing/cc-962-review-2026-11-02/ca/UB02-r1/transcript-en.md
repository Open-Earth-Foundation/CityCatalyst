# UB02-r1 — English review copy

Translation of the recorded Polish interaction. Scores come from revised-judgments.json, not the older translation summary. Missing transport is marked explicitly.

## Turn 1

**User**

My supervisor is asking how our city's emissions changed between the 2023 and 2024 inventories. Can you compare them?

**Climate Advisor**

Yes. For **Brzeg Nadziei**, total emissions increased from **1,50 MtCO₂e in 2023** to **1,80 MtCO₂e in 2024** — that is, by **0,30 MtCO₂e (300 thousand tCO₂e), +20%**.

| Sector | 2023 | 2024 | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Stationary energy | 0,75 MtCO₂e | 0,90 MtCO₂e | +0,15 MtCO₂e |
| Transport | 0,50 MtCO₂e | 0,60 MtCO₂e | +0,10 MtCO₂e |
| Waste | 0,25 MtCO₂e | 0,30 MtCO₂e | +0,05 MtCO₂e |

The emissions structure did not change: stationary energy accounted for **50%**, transport **33,3%**, and waste **16,7%** in both years. The largest sources in 2024 were emissions from residential buildings (scope 1: 0,60 MtCO₂e) and road transport (0,60 MtCO₂e).

Important caveat: both sets of figures have the same coverage and GWP factors (GPC Basic+, AR6), and the data were entered manually or imported. IPPU and AFOLU have no data filled in for either of these years.

## Turn 2

**User**

By how much did this change in tonnes and as a percentage relative to 2023?

**Climate Advisor**

Emissions increased by **300 000 tCO₂e** — from **1 500 000 tCO₂e in 2023** to **1 800 000 tCO₂e in 2024**.

This means an increase of **20% relative to 2023**.
\[
\frac{1{,}800{,}000 - 1{,}500{,}000}{1{,}500{,}000} \times 100\% = 20\%
\]

## Turn 3

**User**

Can I conclude from this that residents used more energy? What should I mention in the note?

**Climate Advisor**

No — emissions alone do not allow you to conclude unequivocally that residents used more energy.

You can, however, write that **emissions from stationary energy increased by 150 000 tCO₂e** (from 750 000 to 900 000 tCO₂e, or by **20%**). Among the largest sources, emissions associated with residential buildings increased:
- scope 1: from 500 000 to 600 000 tCO₂e,
- scope 2: from 250 000 to 300 000 tCO₂e.

**Suggested wording for the note:**

> Between 2023 and 2024, the city's total emissions increased by 300 000 tCO₂e (+20%). Emissions from stationary energy increased by 150 000 tCO₂e (+20%), including an increase in emissions attributed to residential buildings. This result may indicate greater energy consumption, but does not confirm it unequivocally. The change may also have been influenced by, among other things, the fuel mix, electricity emission factors, weather conditions, the number of consumers or data completeness.

It is worth adding that both years have the same inventory coverage (GPC Basic+, AR6), but the sector data were entered manually or imported. To confirm an increase in energy consumption, electricity, gas and fuel consumption data would need to be compared — preferably also per capita and taking heating degree days into account.
