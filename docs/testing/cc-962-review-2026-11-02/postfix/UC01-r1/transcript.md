## Turn 4

**User**

Before I use them, what should I check about their year, geographic coverage and fit for our inventory?

**Climate Advisor**

Check these points before accepting each proposal:

- **Year:** The usable SEEG and EPE candidates match the **2022** inventory year. The Global Energy Monitor and OGIM notation evidence also matches 2022. Avoid sources without a confirmed year match.
- **Geography:** The proposed SEEG and EPE data are matched at **São Paulo city** level—not national values downscaled by population. The notation sources also use city-boundary evidence.
- **GPC row and scope fit:** Confirm the proposal maps to the intended subsector and scope:
  - **SEEG:** Scope 1 fuel combustion.
  - **EPE:** Scope 2 grid electricity and Scope 3 transmission-and-distribution losses.
  - **Global Energy Monitor / OGIM:** only the specified fugitive-emissions rows, with **NO** where no relevant facilities are found within the city boundary.
- **Value type and units:** Check whether the source provides fuel activity data or direct gas emissions, and confirm the activity unit and emissions unit are appropriate for the selected row.
- **Method and boundaries:** Ensure Scope 1 means combustion within São Paulo’s boundary, Scope 2 is purchased grid energy consumed there, and Scope 3 is only electricity transmission-and-distribution losses—without overlap or double counting.
- **Completeness:** Some Energy Industries and Non-Specified Sources rows have no applicable source-backed value. Leave those unfilled or apply an eligible notation key only when it is defensible.

The currently focused proposal is a 2022, city-level **SEEG** estimate for **Scope 1 residential fuel combustion**, so it meets the year and geographic-match checks; it provides emissions by gas rather than a fuel-activity quantity.
