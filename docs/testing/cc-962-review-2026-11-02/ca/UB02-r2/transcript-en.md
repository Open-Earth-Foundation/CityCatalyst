# UB02-r2 — English review copy

Translation of the recorded Polish interaction. Scores come from revised-judgments.json, not the older translation summary. Missing transport is marked explicitly.

## Turn 1

**User**

My supervisor is asking how our city's emissions changed between the 2023 and 2024 inventories. Can you compare them?

**Climate Advisor**

I compared the inventories for Brzeg Nadziei for 2023 and 2024 (both: GPC Basic+ with AR6 GWP values).

**Total emissions increased from 1,50 million tCO₂e in 2023 to 1,80 million tCO₂e in 2024.**
This is an increase of **300 thousand tCO₂e**, or **20%**.

| Sector | 2023 | 2024 | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Stationary energy | 750 thousand tCO₂e | 900 thousand tCO₂e | +150 thousand tCO₂e (+20%) |
| Transport | 500 thousand tCO₂e | 600 thousand tCO₂e | +100 thousand tCO₂e (+20%) |
| Waste | 250 thousand tCO₂e | 300 thousand tCO₂e | +50 thousand tCO₂e (+20%) |

The emissions structure did not change: stationary energy still accounts for **50%** of the total, transport **33,3%**, and waste **16,7%**. The largest individual sources in 2024 were emissions from residential buildings (scope 1) and road transport — **600 thousand tCO₂e** each.

It is worth noting that in both years the data were entered manually or imported; the IPPU and AFOLU sectors have no data filled in.

## Turn 2

**User**

By how much did this change in tonnes and as a percentage relative to 2023?

**Climate Advisor**

Total emissions increased by **300 000 tCO₂e** — from **1 500 000 tCO₂e** in 2023 to **1 800 000 tCO₂e** in 2024.

This means an increase of **20% relative to 2023**.

The increase was also 20% in each sector:
- **Stationary energy:** +150 000 tCO₂e
- **Transport:** +100 000 tCO₂e
- **Waste:** +50 000 tCO₂e

## Turn 3

**User**

Can I conclude from this that residents used more energy? What should I mention in the note?

**Climate Advisor**

Not directly. Based on these data, you can state that **reported emissions from stationary energy increased by 150 000 tCO₂e (20%)** — from 750 000 to 900 000 tCO₂e.

However, it is not safe to state that residents definitely used more energy. The increase in emissions may result from, among other things:
- greater energy consumption,
- a change in fuels or the emissions intensity of electricity,
- changes in data coverage, calculation method or data quality,
- an increase in the number of residents or heated floor area.

**Suggested wording for the note:**

> Between the 2023 and 2024 inventories, the city's total emissions increased by 300 000 tCO₂e, i.e. by 20%. Emissions in the stationary energy sector increased by 150 000 tCO₂e (20%) and remained the largest emissions category. This result indicates an increase in reported energy-related emissions, but does not in itself allow a definitive conclusion that residents' energy consumption increased. Data on fuel and electricity consumption, the number of consumers and the emission factors used are needed to confirm the reasons for the change.

It is also worth noting a limitation of the comparison: both inventories have the same **GPC Basic+** coverage and **AR6** factors, but the data were entered manually or imported, and the IPPU and AFOLU sectors remain unfilled.
