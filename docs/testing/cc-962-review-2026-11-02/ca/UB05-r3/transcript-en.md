## 1. IPPU — industrial processes and product use
First collect information on whether there are plants or processes within the city that cause process emissions, independently of fuel consumption.

**Data to collect**
- the type of activity and installation: e.g. production of cement/lime, glass, ceramics, metals, chemicals, refrigeration and air conditioning;
- the number/type of refrigeration or air conditioning equipment and data on servicing, losses and replenishment of refrigerants (F-gases), if applicable;
- production volume or raw material consumption for industrial processes;
- information on the technical gases and refrigerants used;
- the data year, units, geographical coverage, assumptions and source of each value.

**Potential sources**
- city hall: business records, environmental permits, installation notifications;
- local industrial plants and operators of large facilities;
- the provincial/regional environmental protection inspectorate or national emissions register — where the data are publicly available;
- companies servicing refrigeration and air conditioning, and managers of large buildings;
- official statistics and the national emissions inventory when local data are unavailable.

If, after checking, you confirm that the processes in question **do not occur** within the city boundaries, mark the item as “not occurring” and retain a brief justification. If they occur but the data cannot be obtained, do not enter zero — indicate missing data / not estimated.

## 2. AFOLU — agriculture, forestry and other land use
In GPC, the three required AFOLU groups are:
1. **Livestock**
2. **Land** — including land-use changes, forests, cropland, meadows, wetlands and urban greenery/trees, if relevant
3. **Other sources and non-CO₂ emissions from land** — e.g. fertilization and soil management, depending on local activity

**Data to collect**
- livestock numbers by species and husbandry system;
- land areas by land-use category in 2024 and — if possible — changes relative to previous years;
- data on mineral and organic fertilizers, crops and soil management;
- information on forests, green spaces, tree cover and possible land-cover changes;
- data on drained/wetland areas, if present.

**Potential sources**
- city/municipal office: spatial planning, land and building records, environmental protection and greenery departments;
- local or national agricultural statistics and the agricultural census;
- forest districts, green space managers, surveying authorities and land-cover registers;
- satellite images or land-cover maps — to support verification of areas and changes;
- surveys of farms or farmers when administrative data are lacking.

According to the **internal climate knowledge base**, GPC requires notation keys both for sources that do not occur and for data gaps. It also recommends preferring local and national data, as consistent as possible with the inventory year and city boundary; when they are unavailable, IPCC default values can be applied with the assumptions documented.

## Where to check and fill in the gaps in CityCatalyst
In the **GHGI 2024** inventory, go to the data entry view by sector and open:
- **Sector IV — IPPU**
- **Sector V — AFOLU**

For each missing item, check whether you can set the data status:
- **entered / manually added or uploaded** — when you have data and a calculation method,
- **not occurring** — only when the activity has been verified as absent,
- **not estimated / missing data** — when the source exists but you do not have a reliable value.

When filling in the gaps, also record the source, year, unit, conversion method and all assumptions. This will make the result defensible before it is passed to your supervisor.
