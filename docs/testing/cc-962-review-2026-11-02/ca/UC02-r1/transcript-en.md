## Turn 2

**User**

Let's look at the SEEG proposal for residential buildings, scope 1 (I.1.1). Where do the data come from, what year are they from, and what value do you propose?

**Climate Advisor**

For **I.1.1 — residential buildings, scope 1**, the proposed source is **SEEG (Sistema de Estimativa de Emissão de Gases do Efeito Estufa)**.

- **Data coverage:** national (Brazil)
- **Source year:** 2015
- **Proposed value:** **1 117 504 701 kg CO₂e** (approx. **1.12 MtCO₂e**, 100-year horizon)
- **Components:** CO₂ 1 111 223 686 kg, CH₄ 19 335 kg and N₂O 21 659 kg.

The source does not include a fuel consumption value or an activity unit; it provides emissions directly.

## Turn 3

**User**

Do these data match our city and this category? What cannot be determined from them?

**Climate Advisor**

**The category matches:** the SEEG proposal has been assigned precisely to **I.1.1 — scope 1, residential buildings, fuel combustion within the city boundary**.

**The city fit is limited:** the source has **national**, rather than city, coverage and concerns **2015**, while the active inventory is for **2022**. This means it is not a measurement specific to São Paulo or the inventory year.

The following cannot be determined from this:
- actual residential emissions within the city boundary in 2022;
- fuel consumption or its unit;
- the breakdown of emissions by fuel type, building type or district;
- local emission factors and assumptions used in the conversion.

SEEG provides a direct emissions value for the correct category here, but as national data from an earlier year, it should be treated as a limited representation of the local situation.
