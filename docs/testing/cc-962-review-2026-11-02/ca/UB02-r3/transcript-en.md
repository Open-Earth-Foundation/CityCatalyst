# UB02-r3 — English review copy

Translation of the recorded Polish interaction. Scores come from revised-judgments.json, not the older translation summary. Missing transport is marked explicitly.

## Turn 1

**User**

My supervisor is asking how our city's emissions changed between the 2023 and 2024 inventories. Can you compare them?

**Climate Advisor**

I compared the inventories for Brzeg Nadziei for 2023 and 2024 (both GPC Basic+ and AR6 GWP factors).

**Total emissions increased from 1,5 million tCO₂e to 1,8 million tCO₂e** — by **300 thousand tCO₂e**, or **20%**.

| Sector | 2023 | 2024 | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Stationary energy | 750 thousand tCO₂e | 900 thousand tCO₂e | +150 thousand tCO₂e (+20%) |
| Transport | 500 thousand tCO₂e | 600 thousand tCO₂e | +100 thousand tCO₂e (+20%) |
| Waste | 250 thousand tCO₂e | 300 thousand tCO₂e | +50 thousand tCO₂e (+20%) |

The emissions structure remained the same: stationary energy accounts for **50%**, transport for **33,3%**, and waste for **16,7%** of total emissions. The largest items in 2024 were emissions from residential buildings (scope 1: 600 thousand tCO₂e; scope 2: 300 thousand tCO₂e) and road transport (600 thousand tCO₂e).

It is worth noting that in both years the data were entered manually or uploaded, and the IPPU and AFOLU sectors have no data filled in.

## Turn 2

**User**

By how much did this change in tonnes and as a percentage relative to 2023?

**Climate Advisor**

Total emissions **increased by 300 000 tCO₂e**, from 1 500 000 tCO₂e in 2023 to 1 800 000 tCO₂e in 2024.

This means an increase of **20% relative to 2023**.

## Turn 3

**User**

Can I conclude from this that residents used more energy? What should I mention in the note?

**Climate Advisor**

Not directly. Based on these data, you can state that **emissions attributed to stationary energy increased by 150 000 tCO₂e (20%)**. In particular, emissions from residential buildings increased:

- **scope 1:** by 100 000 tCO₂e (from 500 000 to 600 000),
- **scope 2:** by 50 000 tCO₂e (from 250 000 to 300 000).

This **may** indicate greater energy or fuel consumption by residents, but the emissions inventory alone does not confirm this. The increase may also result from, among other things, changes in electricity or fuel emission factors, data coverage, methodology or reporting completeness.

**Suggested wording for the note:**

> Between 2023 and 2024, emissions from stationary energy increased by 150 thousand tCO₂e, i.e. by 20%. The increase primarily concerns residential buildings, both direct emissions (scope 1) and indirect emissions associated with purchased electricity (scope 2). However, the result alone does not allow a definitive conclusion that residents' energy consumption increased. It requires verification of fuel and energy consumption data, emission factors and the comparability of the methodology between years.

It is also worth noting that both sets of data were entered manually or uploaded, and the IPPU and AFOLU sectors are not filled in.
