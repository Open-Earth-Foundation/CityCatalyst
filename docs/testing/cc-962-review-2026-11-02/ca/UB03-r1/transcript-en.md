# UB03-r1 — English review copy

Translation of the recorded Polish interaction. Scores come from revised-judgments.json, not the older translation summary. Missing transport is marked explicitly.

## Turn 1

**User**

In the results for 2024, I want to understand stationary energy better. How much falls under scope 1, and how much under scope 2?

**Climate Advisor**

In the inventory for 2024, stationary energy totals **900 000 tCO₂e** (50% of the city's total emissions):

- **Scope 1:** **600 000 tCO₂e**
- **Scope 2:** **300 000 tCO₂e**

Both values primarily concern residential buildings. Scope 1 is therefore twice as large as scope 2.

## Turn 2

**User**

How can I explain the difference between these scopes to my supervisor in plain language?

**Climate Advisor**

Most simply:

- **Scope 1** means emissions generated **directly on site** — for example, when residents heat their homes with gas, coal or heating oil. In stationary energy, this is **600 thousand tCO₂e**.
- **Scope 2** means emissions associated with **purchased electricity or heat**, which are generated elsewhere but consumed in the city. This is **300 thousand tCO₂e**.

You can tell your supervisor:

> “In scope 1, we count fuel combustion in buildings, for example in furnaces and boilers. In scope 2, we count emissions from the production of electricity or heat purchased by residents and businesses. Scope 1 therefore shows local fuel combustion, and scope 2 — the emissions footprint of energy supplied from outside.”

In the results for 2024, **scope 1** is the bigger problem — it is twice as high as scope 2.

## Turn 3

**User**

Do these figures add up to the entire energy result, and does that mean we are not missing any data there?

**Climate Advisor**

Yes — for stationary energy, these two values add up to the reported result:

- Scope 1: **600 thousand tCO₂e**
- Scope 2: **300 thousand tCO₂e**
- **Total: 900 thousand tCO₂e**

This corresponds to the entire result for the stationary energy sector in the 2024 inventory.

However, this does not allow an unequivocal conclusion that no source data are missing in this sector. In the inventory summary, stationary energy is filled in, and the missing sectors are indicated elsewhere: **IPPU and AFOLU**. The stationary energy values come from data entered manually or uploaded, so their completeness depends on the coverage of those data.
